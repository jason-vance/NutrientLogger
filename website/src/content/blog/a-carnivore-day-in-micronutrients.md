A few months ago I wrote about [the carnivore micronutrient gaps nobody talks about](/blog/carnivore-diet-micronutrient-gaps). That post made the case in general terms. This one shows the numbers.

I logged two days of carnivore eating in Nutrient Logger and looked at every vitamin and mineral. Every value comes from the USDA's food data, the same data the app ships with. The targets are the standard RDAs for a man aged 31 to 50. I'll note where women's targets change the picture.

## Day 1: the muscle-meat day

This is the day most people mean when they say "carnivore":

- **Breakfast:** 3 scrambled eggs, 3 slices of bacon
- **Lunch:** 8 oz ground beef (80/20)
- **Dinner:** 12 oz ribeye with a tablespoon of butter

About 2,000 calories and 176 g of protein. It's a good day of eating by most standards.

## Day 2: the same day with two swaps

Same breakfast. Then two changes:

- **Lunch:** the same 8 oz of ground beef, with **1 oz of beef liver** mixed in. At that amount you can barely taste it.
- **Dinner:** **8 oz salmon and a can of sardines** instead of the ribeye.

About 1,800 calories and 166 g of protein. Nothing exotic, nothing you can't buy at a normal grocery store.

## What the swaps fixed

One ounce of liver and one can of fish did a lot of work.

- **Vitamin A** went from 44% to 306%, almost all of it from the ounce of liver.
- **Vitamin D** went from 22% to 252%. Salmon and sardines are two of the few foods with real vitamin D in them.
- **Folate** doubled, and **copper** went from 70% to over five times the target. Both come from the liver.
- **Calcium** more than doubled, from the sardines' soft bones.
- **Thiamin** went from 41% to 97%, mostly from the salmon.

This is the nose-to-tail argument in one table. The gap isn't really between carnivore and other diets. It's between eating the whole animal and eating only muscle meat.

## What barely moved

Three gaps survived the swaps, and they're the ones worth knowing about.

**Magnesium: 35% → 44%.** This is the one I'd watch first. Beef has about 20 mg per 100 g, and fish isn't much better. Neither day comes close, and no reasonable amount of meat will. Magnesium matters for sleep, muscle cramps and recovery, which are complaints that often get blamed on "adaptation."

**Manganese: 4% → 13%.** Muscle meat has almost none. The exception is shellfish: 3 oz of mussels has about 5.8 mg, roughly two and a half days' worth. If you eat seafood, mussels close this gap on their own.

**Vitamin C: 0% → 10%.** Cooked meat has essentially none. The carnivore argument that you need less vitamin C without carbs competing for the same transporters has some science behind it. I covered it in the earlier post. Either way, don't assume you're covered. Track it.

**Vitamin K** sits around 13 to 15% on both days. Most of what animal foods have is K2, which shows up in hard cheese, egg yolks and liver. The USDA data barely measures K2, so treat this number as uncertain rather than as a real gap.

## Three things the table doesn't show

**Women's targets change iron.** The RDA for iron is 18 mg for women aged 19 to 50, compared with 8 mg for men. Day 1 goes from 181% to 81%, and Day 2 lands at 75%. Magnesium looks a little better against a woman's 320 mg target, at 46% and 57%, but it's still well short.

**Liver is a dose, not a meal.** One ounce gave 2,170 µg of vitamin A. Three ounces would be about 6,500 µg, more than double the 3,000 µg upper limit for preformed vitamin A. An ounce a day, or a full serving once or twice a week, gets the benefit without the risk.

**Sodium is before added salt.** Both days come in around 1,200 to 1,500 mg before you salt anything. Most carnivore eaters salt heavily, and many need to. Potassium, at 64 to 71%, is the other half of that balance.

## How to check your own

These are my two days, not yours. Your cuts, portions and how often you eat seafood will move every number in that table.
